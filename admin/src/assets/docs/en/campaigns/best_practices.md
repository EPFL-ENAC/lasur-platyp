## Best Practices

### When Configuring Measures

1. **Review measures regularly**: Update as new initiatives launch or old ones end
2. **Consider both personal and professional travel**: Many companies focus on commuting but professional travel is equally important
3. **Be specific with custom measures**: Clear, descriptive labels help participants understand available options

### When Setting Up Campaigns

1. **Choose meaningful names**: Include the year or season for clarity
2. **Set realistic end dates**: Allow sufficient time for participation
3. **Define workplaces carefully**: Accurate locations enable better travel time and emissions calculations
4. **Consider campaign-specific measures**: Use them to test new initiatives before organisation-wide rollout

### When sharing the questionnaire with staff

Based on tests carried out in 2025 with Mobilyse, here are a few tips to increase the response rate to the questionnaire, highlighting the key points regarding communication with staff:

-	**The ‘why’ behind the initiative**: communicate clearly about the initiative: 1) why this survey, 2) how the results will be used, and 3) what benefits it will bring them (the analysis will help improve support for sustainable mobility for employees, based on their needs and wishes; in particular, we are asking for their views and how best to support them; etc.)
  
-    **The choice of communication channel** (tailored to each audience: office-based staff, workshop-based staff, etc.) and management support (mentioning it in other meetings, via line managers, etc.)
  
-    **Choosing the right time to communicate about the campaign** (timing it to coincide with a key moment for the company, such as a relocation or a corporate seminar… and avoiding a launch on a Monday or Friday)
  
-    **The duration of the campaign** (1 to 2 weeks is the ideal duration)
  
-    **Sharing the results** – ensure proper feedback to colleagues: share a few key takeaways and what will be done with them; consider an iterative process to measure impacts and refine measures; your colleagues must be convinced of their contribution
  
-    **Taking into account your colleagues’ comfort with an online/smartphone questionnaire** (for example, identify a contact person available to help complete the questionnaire; make a computer available for a day to complete the questionnaire if people do not work at a desk)

-    **Encouraging participation** – as a team during a 15-minute coffee break with complimentary croissants, offering a voucher worth a few francs for a bike service or other service… The more the information is communicated within small teams (by a line manager, for example), the more likely people are to take part.
